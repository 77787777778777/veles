*veles 0.2.9, 2026-11-17.*

Subagents no longer die at 15 minutes. A `delegate_task` child runs
until it is done, unless a time limit was set for it.

## Fixes

### No fixed time limit on subagents

- Until now every `delegate_task` child was killed after 900 seconds (15
  minutes). No argument or setting could change this, and nothing told
  the model about it. Work longer than that (a build, a test suite, a
  model that thinks for minutes per step) was killed partway through.
  The parent got only "child timed out after 900s", with no summary, and
  usually started the same work again.
- Now a child runs until it finishes or is cancelled. Each tool call and
  each provider call still has its own timeout, so a single hung step
  cannot hang the child forever.
- This applies to children on other nodes too. The node that sent the
  work now waits for a child with no limit for as long as it runs. TCP
  keepalive notices a peer that has gone away.
- A child that runs for a long time stays counted in its node's load.
  Without this fix, a child that used the `nodes` tool after running
  for 18 minutes would have deleted its own entry from the count; the
  15-minute kill had hidden that.

### A time limit, when you want one

- `delegate_task` takes `time_limit_minutes` for one call, and
  `[agent] subagent_time_limit_minutes` sets a default for all children.
  Both are unset by default; 0 means no limit.
- Shortly before the limit, the child gets a session mail telling it to
  wrap up and write its summary. The mail arrives at its next tool
  result, and the child is warned a fifth of the limit before it, at
  most five minutes before.
- At the limit the child is asked to stop. It is killed only if it has
  not exited after 20 seconds.
- The parent's verdict names the limit and includes the child's last
  message as `last_message`, so a stopped child's work is not lost.

### A stopped child no longer leaves processes behind

- A child stopped by a cancel or a time limit now cancels its own turn
  and ends its background shells, each with its whole process tree.
  Before, it was killed outright, and builds and test daemons started in
  its background shells kept running; one ran for 2.5 hours after its
  child was gone.
- A child whose turn ends normally ends its background shells too. Once
  the child has exited, nothing can read their output.
- On Windows, a child and everything it started run in one job object,
  which is ended when the child stops.
